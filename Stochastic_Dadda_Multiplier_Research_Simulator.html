Dadda rows R₁,R₂→Sobol SNGs→Balanced MUX→2 × density × rangeExperimental
The conventional binary Dadda path is ground truth. The stochastic path encodes A and B, applies bitwise AND, and estimates the product from measured density. The experimental hybrid encodes the two final Dadda rows, then applies a balanced MUX average and ×2 decode; independence and finite-stream errors remain measurable.
A stochastic MUX is not a drop-in binary final adder. It estimates an arithmetic average only under a balanced select stream and suitable independence. Hardware area, power, and timing require RTL synthesis and power analysis; browser cycles and execution time do not establish them.

Stochastic addition workspace

MUX and OR waveforms

X┐2:1 MUX→ Z = SX + (1−S)Y
For a balanced S independent of X and Y: P(Z=1)=(P(X=1)+P(Y=1))/2, so decode the sum as 2P(Z=1). Correlation or select imbalance can bias the result. OR is a separate architecture and does not generally implement ordinary addition.

Sobol SNG research module

Definition and diagnostics

One-dimensional Sobol construction: direction numbers vj=232−j, j=1…32; initial direction numerators mj=1. For zero-based index n, compute Gray code g=n⊕(n≫1), then qn=XOR(vj for each set bit gj), and map to [0,1) as q/232. Thus q0=0. Stream bit is 1 iff x>q. Phase means skip to index n=phase; it does not create independence.

Default unipolar normalization is x=A/(2N−1), compared against Sobol q in [0,1). Finite L quantizes measured density in increments of 1/L. Low discrepancy may improve finite-sample behavior but does not guarantee unbiased correlated operations.
